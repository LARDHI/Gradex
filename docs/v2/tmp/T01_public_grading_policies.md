**Unspecified or inconsistent rules (AS-10):**
- **NCR has several treatments.** UTSC: the table lists NCR with no value and a note says NCR and NC% are failing grades with no credit; the CR/NCR text says a student-elected NCR is **not** a failure and **not** in GPA; but for courses graded CR/NCR by design, NCR counts as F (0.0) in GPA. Registrar: CR/NCR generally not in GPA, and "NC%" (0.0) where it is. A&S: elected NCR is outside GPA. iSchool: NCR has no numeric value and is not in GPA, yet **counts like an FZ for academic warning and probation.**
- **GPA denominator wording differs by division** (full credits taken at UTSC; courses in which grade points were earned at A&S). Whether an F "earned grade points" in the A&S wording is not stated, and whether a half-credit course counts as 0.5 or 1 in that count is not stated.
- **Graduate points.** The 2020 policy's graduate table has no grade points and its transcript section asks for no graduate GPA; the 1998 policy and the registrar guide list points (A+ 4.0 down to B- 2.7, FZ 0.0), and iSchool says FZ contributes 0 to GPA. The registrar graduate table also prints points for C and D rows that have no letter.
- 2020 policy section B 4.3.1 points to "A 1.2.3", which does not exist in the text (Part A has only 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.3 and 1.4 in this area, and the 4-point table is in A 1.2.1).
- A&S says "four types" of averages and lists three (sessional, annual, cumulative).
- Courses at UTSC, FAS and UTM are "normally" included in UTSC averages; the exceptions are not stated. The Second Attempt for Credit rule is explicitly not retroactive before September 2025.
- iSchool links to a 2012 version of the policy while the 2020 policy is current.
- No rounding rule and no UTSC repeat rule found.

**Interactions between rules that can change one calculation:**
1. **Percentage boundaries and points:** 84 gives A- (3.7) and 85 gives A (4.0); 89, 85 and 90 all give 4.0, so a rise from 85 to 90 changes the letter and not the points.
2. **A+ equal to A in points** while staying distinct on the transcript.
3. **Failed attempt stays in GPA after a pass:** a failed first attempt (0.0) keeps counting after a later pass, so a retake raises the denominator (every attempt counts), unlike replace rules in P05, P07 and P10.
4. **Second Attempt for Credit:** for a passed course repeated with a higher mark, the first attempt drops out of GPA and credit, but only up to 2.0 credits in date order and only for attempts from September 2025; a later higher mark may not free the first attempt once the 2.0 credits are used.
5. **Extra second attempt:** a repeat of a passed course with a lower or equal mark stays Extra, so it is invisible to GPA.
6. **NCR across divisions:** the same code is outside GPA, inside GPA at 0.0, or a standing event depending on division and on whether the student elected it.
7. **Half credits as weights:** a half course counts half in the weighted sum; with the A&S denominator wording, it is unclear whether it counts half in the denominator.
8. **Averages and standing in parallel:** standing uses CGPA together with annual or sessional GPA, with a different threshold by programme (1.50, 1.70, 2.0, 2.50), so one record can be in good standing in one programme and on probation in another.
9. **Graduate cliff:** 69% is FZ (0.0) and 70% is B- (2.7).
10. **Transfer and out-of-division courses:** transfer credits, Extra courses and passed Pass/Fail courses give credit or none but never enter GPA, while courses from UTM, UTSC and Daniels taken by an A&S student do.
11. **Exceptions by programme:** a 60% pass mark in some professional programmes changes what counts as a failure for the same letter table.

**What V1 could not represent:** half credits (0.5); percentage input; the graduate FZ scale as entered (V1 requires a letter F in a custom table, so FZ would have to be relabelled F); CR/NCR and NC% as codes whose GPA effect depends on division and election; P/FL with FL% at 0.0; Extra status and the Second Attempt for Credit rule (a date-dependent, credit-capped swap); sessional, annual and cumulative averages together (V1 has semester and cumulative); programme-specific standing thresholds and suspension sequences; per-division pass marks. **V1 can store the 13-letter undergraduate scale as a custom table** (A+ 4.00 and A 4.00 are allowed because points need not differ), and V1's "every attempt counts" retake rule and the rule that only F fails are consistent with the failed-course rules read.

**Unspecified or inconsistent rules (AS-10):**
- **Summer load:** the regulation says 12 credit hours; the FAQ says 9 (more for a graduating student with approval).
- **Course withdrawals:** the regulation allows up to two courses per semester (four in total, a summer course counting half, rounded up in the student's favour); the FAQ says one course per semester, as long as at least 12 hours remain. Both agree on three semester withdrawals in total (summer not counted).
- **The FAQ GPA example labels a 96 as "A" with 5.00**, while Article 28, Appendix B and the 2020 notice give 96 as A+ 5.00. This resolves the question in the earlier version of this entry (the points follow the score band; the FAQ symbol is a labelling slip) but the FAQ itself is not corrected.
- **Retake treatment of a failed attempt** is not stated; the honours rule ("no failure in any course") suggests failed attempts stay relevant after a retake, but that is an inference.
- **Rounding:** only worked examples; no rule text. Whether the overall-grade and honours cut-offs (4.50, 3.75, 4.75, 4.25 ...) are compared with the rounded or the unrounded average is not stated.
- **Dismissal:** the Article allows a fourth chance by decision of the University Council; the executive rule grants a "third opportunity" to a student who can reach the minimum by earning 48 points in 12 credit hours (which would need an average of 4.0 on those hours); the two do not obviously describe the same step.
- D2 points, the points of AL, and the rules for fractional units are not given.
- The regulation is a 2015 English copy hosted by a college; the notice is a 2020 temporary measure; the FAQ is undated (page footer 2025). Which is current for 2026 is not established.

**Interactions between rules that can change one calculation:**
1. **Band edges:** a mark of 84.99 gives B (4.00) and 85.00 gives B+ (4.50); marks can be non-integers, so the edge depends on how the final mark is rounded, which is not stated.
2. **F is worth 1.00, not 0:** a failed course adds points to the numerator and credits to the denominator (as in V1's 5.0 presets); on the 4.0 table F is 0.0, so the same record gives different averages on the two scales.
3. **DN as a failure with F's points** even though the student never sat the exam, and a college council can lift the denial (attendance still at least 50%).
4. **Time-driven conversion:** IC and AL become F after one semester (unless an excuse or a postponement), and the F then counts in both averages; the average changes with the calendar.
5. **Withdrawal W:** outside the averages, but limited to 3 semester excuses (never 3 in a row) and 4 courses in a career; the withdrawn period still counts toward the maximum stay, so W can lead to dismissal for time.
6. **GPA-dependent grade recording (2019/2020 only):** a course grade was recorded only if its weight was at least the student's current cumulative average, otherwise NP; a mark under 60 (70 for graduate students) became NF. The semester and cumulative averages were then recomputed from the recorded courses only (notice example 1: 3.24 over 60 hours plus 24.75 points over 6 hours gives 3.32). The same score could be a letter for one student and NP for another. NP / NF stay out of the GPA until graduation and do not affect honours eligibility; a student with no GPA, and all first-common-year courses, got NP / NF only.
7. **Exceptions for that semester:** the semester GPA was not counted for academic warnings, deprivation was waived, and withdrawals did not count against the limits or the maximum stay.
8. **Cumulative thresholds act at several points:** graduation minimum (2.00, or 2.75 in two colleges), warnings, dismissal, enrolment load limits and honours all use the cumulative average, so one CGPA can be a warning in one college and not in another.
9. **Honours versus retakes:** any failed course, even one retaken and passed, may block honours.
10. **Where a course came from:** courses moved inside KSU count in the cumulative average; equivalent courses from other universities and visiting-student courses do not; preparatory-year grades count.
11. **Two scales on one record:** overall grade bands are stated out of 5.00 and out of 4.00 (for example Very good 3.75 or 2.75), so the displayed average and the band depend on the scale chosen.
12. **Yearly-system colleges:** a second-round pass replaces F with D2 (a code, not a number), and a repeat year restudies only failed courses.

**What V1 could not represent:** percentage input with non-integer marks and 60/40 or 80/20 weights; DN, IC, IP, W, AL, NP, NF and D2 as codes with deadlines; the GPA-dependent NP rule; two weight scales on one record; the honours conditions, warnings and dismissal; per-college graduation minimums; load limits tied to the average. **V1 can store the main scale exactly**: its built-in "5.0 Plus Only" table equals KSU's 5.00 column, "4.0 Plus Only" equals the 4.00 column, F counts in the average, there are no minus grades, and half-up rounding to two decimals fits every worked example; credits stay within V1's 1 to 9 range in all examples seen.

**Interactions between rules that can change one calculation:**
1. **Inverted direction** with every "best" and "lowest" notion, with prediction and with any target: a target is a ceiling (at most 2.0), and "best semester" is the lowest number.
2. **Credit weighting with a numeric pass threshold:** a module at 4.0 passes and earns full credits even though it raises the average.
3. **Rounding or cut-off with band limits:** the same weighted average can sit in a different classification band depending on a rule that is not written down (see above).

7. **Two classification schemes at one university:** a module grade and the overall grade carry different descriptors for the same number.

- **Section 7.7.2** says the minimum "CPI (SPI)" may be relaxed in the first semester, mixing the cumulative and the semester index.

**Interactions between rules that can change one calculation:**
1. **Retake replacement with credits and CPI:** the old grade drops out of CPI but stays on the transcript, and eligibility to repeat a D depends on the CPI, the programme and the semester load.
2. **Parallel calculations:** the CPI and the counts of E, F and X are separate tests; one course can end a programme without changing the CPI much, and X grades never enter the CPI at all.
3. **Calendar-driven conversion:** an I becomes F on a calendar date and then counts in SPI and CPI.
4. **Opt-in at registration:** an extra course counts in the CPI only if the student asks for that when registering; otherwise it is S/X and invisible to the CPI.
5. **Waiver credits:** a waiver (W) can satisfy a requirement with zero weight, so credits and CPI weights differ.
6. **Thresholds with discretion:** the same CPI (for example 6.2) can mean a warning, a relaxed first semester, or termination depending on the semester number, the programme and an SPGC decision.
7. **Two points per step:** D to C is two points and E to F is two points, so one grade step moves a 9-credit course by 18 grade points, and A\* gives no extra over A.
8. **Marks and CPI equivalence:** admission thresholds are written as percent or CPI, with 55 percent equal to 5.5.
9. **IIT Bombay (transcripts):** AP and AA are both 10, FF and FR are both 0 with different meanings; a term with only a zero-credit course prints SPI 0.00/10; project credits are averaged separately and together (CPI Courses and CPI Overall).

**What V1 could not represent:** points above 5.00 (the whole 10-point scale; V1 custom points stop at 5.00); the letter A\* (V1 letters allow only A to Z with an optional + or -); S, X and I as codes with a calendar deadline; replace-on-retake with conditions on the old grade; credits above 9 and zero-credit courses; opt-in CPI counting; waiver credits; programme-specific CPI minimums with warnings and termination rules; E, F and X counting rules; separate course and project averages. **Derived observation (my arithmetic, not an IITK rule):** because the CPI is a linear average, halving every point (A 5.00, B 4.00, C 3.00, D 2.00, E 1.00, F 0.00) would give exactly half the CPI, but V1 treats every grade except F as passing, while the manual requires E to be repeated, so the rescaled table would still not match the rules.

**Interactions between rules that can change one calculation:**
1. **Pending grade replaced later:** a 4.0 counts in the GWA until it is removed, then the 4 drops out and only the 3 or 5 counts, while the transcript keeps the 4. The result changes after the semester ends.
2. **Attribution to a different semester:** a final grade replacing an INC is counted in the semester in which the removal is made, not the semester of the course.
3. **Calendar-driven conversion:** a 4 not removed in one year becomes 5 on its own, so the GWA changes with the date even with no new work.
4. **Retake with the old grade kept:** a 5 stays in the GWA when the course is re-enrolled and passed, and the re-enrolled units add to the total, so a retake raises the denominator (every attempt counts, unlike P05, P07 and P10 where a retake replaces).
5. **Waiving removal by re-enrolling:** re-enrolling a 4 within the year keeps the 4 in the GWA together with the new grade.
6. **Uneven scale and direction:** 3.0 to 4.0 is one full step after steps of 0.25, so one conditional grade moves the average much more than one grade point below it; lower is better, so "best semester" and any target are lower-is-better.
7. **Parallel calculation:** scholastic standing is computed from the share of units below 3.00, not from the GWA, so one course can change standing and not the GWA, or the reverse.
8. **Different deadlines for different purposes:** an INC or 4 must be removed within one academic year for the record, but before the end of the semester in which it was incurred for honorific-scholar status (UP Cebu).
9. **Excluded courses and periods:** PE and NSTP are excluded while PI 100 is included; one whole semester (second semester AY 2019-2020) was excluded at UP Diliman.
10. **Residence condition:** to complete an INC or remove a 4 the student must be enrolled or registered in residence, so a grade can stay pending because of enrolment status.
